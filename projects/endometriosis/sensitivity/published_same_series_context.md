# Published same-series comparator and nomenclature conflict (September 28, 2026)

Original paper: Yamagata et al. 2014, PLOS One, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0083612 . Expression series: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE47360 . The paper is **prior analysis of the same cohort**, not an external benchmark or independent replication of this project's result. Its Results under "Genome-wide Transcriptome Analysis" report 2 genes up and 8 down in a same-tissue eutopic disease-group comparison versus 498 up and 329 down in its ectopic-cyst versus eutopic comparison, using its own thresholds. It reports RT-PCR follow-up of NR5A1, STAR, STRA6 and HSD17B2 in cultured cells and tissues. These gene names are published prior art, **not new markers** found by this work. These counts use the paper's gene-mapping and threshold procedure; our sensitivity is a Hedges-g probe-sign comparison and cannot be compared as an accuracy metric.

**Source-naming mismatch.** The paper's abstract calls the eutopic *with-endometriosis* group `euESCa` and *without-endometriosis* group `euESCb`. But the nine GEO full GSM records and titles call `euESCa1-3` the **non-endometriosis** controls and `euESCb1-3` the **endometriosis** eutopic cases. The paper's opening group definitions and its later `euESCa`/`euESCb` contrast labels therefore cannot be bound to GSMs just by letter. The `choESC` cells remain ectopic ovarian-cyst samples, and the GEO disease/tissue characteristics are internally consistent across the nine records. The paper does not provide a GSM-by-donor ID map; its statement that a pair of eutopic and cyst samples came from one individual for a targeted methylation assay does **not** identify which expression GSMs, if any, are paired. Preserve the mismatch and avoid claiming donor-level independence or a resolved per-GSM published gene contrast.

The source-based nine-GSM tissue-scope analysis in this directory uses explicit GEO disease and tissue fields, not the paper's letter names. It demonstrates sensitivity to removing ectopic samples, but it is post-exposure and tiny. It has no standalone published same-task comparator beat or independently novel gene.
